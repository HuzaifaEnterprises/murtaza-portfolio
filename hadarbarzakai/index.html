How a company page works

Four moves, repeated for each role.

1

Read the role

The job description and the company's own site, for their exact words and priorities.

2

Wear their brand

Their colors, fonts and phrases, so it feels like part of their world.

3

Answer their questions

The screening questions, plus what the CEO would ask on the first call.

4

Place the proof

10 to 15 samples: match the ones that fit. Fewer: build relatable samples, labeled as samples.

The part that decides everything

A page nobody opens solves nothing.

The page is the answer. The delivery is what gets it read. Three habits that fix the viewing problem:

1

Send it to a person

Do not rely on the form. Put the link in a short note to the hiring manager, the CEO, or someone on the team, so a human opens it before a filter decides.

2

Make the click worth it

The message names the company's own priority in the first line and the page answers it. A link that mentions their problem gets opened.

3

Use your strength

You have said you shine in conversation. Send the page before the call, so the first ten minutes start where a screening call usually ends.

Example note, two linesHi [name], I saw you are hiring a Tech Chief of Staff and that [their priority]. I put together a short page that answers how I would approach it, with examples: [link]

Same link goes in the application itself. The form is still filled in, but now it carries a page that does the explaining.
